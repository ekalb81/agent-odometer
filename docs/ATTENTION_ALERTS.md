# Agent attention and transcript events

Agent attention in Settings starts with every category off. Explicit preferences select turn start, recorded input request, tool completion/failure, turn completion, and interruption/rollback events. Matching is limited to provider and normalized tool category; prompts and tool arguments are never matched or shown in notices.

These are observations from already-parsed retained turn/tool metadata, not process supervision. Working means a turn opened or a tool completed; idle means a recorded turn completed. Error means a recorded tool failure, not failure of the whole task. Waiting requires a pending `request_user_input`, `functions.request_user_input`, or `AskUserQuestion` tool observation. Missing signals and unsupported states stay unknown. Aborted or rolled-back turns are unknown rather than guessed failures. A provider without those signals cannot offer that state.

The backend examines the latest 50 retained turns and 200 tool observations per published session; partial slices are labeled. It retains at most 100 session observations, expires them after the configured 30–3600 seconds, and rejects future timestamps. The UI offers one, five, or fifteen minutes. Missing, purged, superseded, and archived sources do not remain current. Existing history appears after a scan or append while enabled; enabling does not trigger a corpus reparse or replay earlier alerts.

Notice text contains a fixed provider label, opaque session reference, fixed event category, fixed source description, and observation time. It excludes session titles, prompts, paths, tool names/arguments, credentials, and model content. In-app notices work without desktop notification permission; the separate permission button is explicit. Desktop notification availability depends on the OS/webview. Odometer must remain open.

The private, versioned `attention-v1.json` stores preferences, hashed deduplication identities, per-session event-time watermarks, and the last notice time. It never stores transcript bodies. Updates use optimistic preference revisions, a separate exclusive lock, and atomic file replacement. Unreadable, malformed, oversized, newer-version, locked, or unwritable state suppresses notices and preserves existing data. Future persisted notice/watermark times also make the service unavailable rather than silently suppressing alerts until that date. A successful explicit preference save restores a write failure after its cause is resolved. This store is independent of the usage ledger and quota notification store.

At most one notice is emitted per 30 seconds across the app. Suppressed events are consumed, not delayed. Older or same-time late signals cannot replay a notice after their watermark; a completion without a distinct recorded time may update displayed evidence without producing a new notice. Restart/enabling creates a new observation boundary and does not replay a backlog. Deduplication is bounded to 512 hashes and 100 active session watermarks; additional active sessions beyond that bound are not notified until a slot expires. Closing/reopening the UI likewise seeds existing notice IDs without replay.

There are no parser/accounting changes, provider hooks, new Tauri capabilities, process controls, network requests, or credential access in this feature.
